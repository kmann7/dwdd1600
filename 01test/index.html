Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the responsible, ethical, and safe use of technology, the internet, and digital communication platforms. It includes respecting others online, protecting personal information, avoiding harmful behavior like cyberbullying, and using digital tools appropriately and legally.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law refers to the rules, rights, and legal responsibilities that govern the use of technology and the internet. It includes issues such as copyright, privacy, cybercrime, hacking, plagiarism, online harassment, and the legal use of digital content and software.

What is AUP?

An AUP, or Acceptable Use Policy, is a set of rules that explains how users are allowed to use a company's, school's, or organization's technology, internet, or network systems. It outlines acceptable behavior, prohibited activities, and consequences for misuse.

What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is the act of using someone else’s words, ideas, work, or creations without giving proper credit or permission. It can include copying text, images, code, music, or AI-generated content and presenting it as your own original work.

What is Academic Dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any form of cheating or unethical behavior in an educational setting, such as plagiarism, copying work, or using unauthorized resources.

It violates school policies and undermines fairness, trust, and academic integrity.

What are the rules for AI Generated Content?

AI-generated content rules vary depending on the context, but the main principles are consistent: human creativity is usually required for copyright protection, many schools and workplaces require disclosure of AI use, and deceptive uses like deepfakes, impersonation, fake endorsements, scams, or nonconsensual synthetic media are increasingly illegal or heavily restricted. Most platforms also prohibit misleading or spammy AI content, while companies and universities often require human review, transparency, and fact-checking before publishing AI-assisted work. In general, using AI as a tool is acceptable, but passing fully AI-generated work off as entirely human, especially in academic, professional, or commercial settings, can create ethical, legal, or policy issues.
