How Ski Length Works

A basic ski length recommendation is usually based on a skier's height, weight, and ability level. Shorter skis are often easier to turn, while longer skis can feel more stable at speed.

For many skiers, a good starting point is a ski that reaches somewhere between the chin and the top of the head. A more experienced skier may choose a longer ski, while a newer skier may feel more comfortable on something shorter.

Basic Ski Sizing Steps

  1. Start with your height to find a general ski length range.
  2. Think about your weight and whether you may need a shorter or longer ski.
  3. Consider your skier type, such as beginner, intermediate, or advanced.
  4. Compare your result with a trusted ski sizing guide before making a final choice.

Common Ski Terms

Ski Length
The measurement of the ski from tip to tail, usually listed in centimeters.
Skier Type
A general description of a skier's ability level, such as beginner, intermediate, or advanced.
DIN Setting
The release setting on ski bindings that helps determine how easily a boot releases from the binding.

Helpful Ski Sizing Notes

Ski sizing is not only about height. A skier's strength, confidence, preferred terrain, and riding style can all make a difference. This is why ski sizing should be viewed as a helpful starting point.

If you are new to skiing, it is usually better to choose a ski that is easier to control. A slightly shorter ski can often feel less intimidating for a beginner.
